# Humor and Voice Bible

## 1. Humor is a gameplay system

The desired humor comes from the collision between real danger, absurd rules, petty authority, emotionally sincere characters, and a machine that thinks every disaster is content. It should not feel like a generic comedian narrating button presses.

The comedy must do at least one useful job:

- reveal a rule;
- characterize a person, faction, god, zone, item, or institution;
- remember a player choice;
- reward or complicate unusual play;
- relieve tension after danger;
- set up a callback;
- turn a systemic outcome into a story the player will repeat;
- make an otherwise disposable item worth reading once.

A line that does none of these is noise, even if it is funny in isolation.

## 2. Tone target

The default blend is:

- cosmic spectacle treated like bad office administration;
- brutal situations punctured by embarrassingly specific details;
- grandiose announcements about trivial failures;
- intelligent characters bickering from incompatible priorities;
- literal interpretations of magical rules;
- items whose powers and descriptions tell miniature stories;
- systems that promote a joke into a real mechanical consequence;
- occasional sincerity that is not immediately destroyed by a punchline.

The game can be dark, profane, and strange, but humor primarily targets power, vanity, bureaucracy, predatory commerce, divine entitlement, institutional incompetence, monsters, and the player's harmlessly ridiculous choices. It does not target real-world vulnerable identities, trauma disclosures, or the player's appearance, voice, name, accessibility settings, or actual skill level.

## 3. Comedy pillars

### Stakes make jokes land

Combat and choices matter, but the game does not need global countdowns, lost equipment, or erased progress. Spotlight, promoted enemies, zone consequences, missed bonus rewards, faction reactions, and remembered indignities give the joke a real object.

### Precision beats randomness

"You failed" is not funny. "You were outmaneuvered by a fungus that has no recognized nervous system" is a story. Commentary should name the enemy family, method, place, previous boast, relevant item, or exact pattern when that specificity improves the line.

### Callbacks create intimacy

The game remembers the low-tier creature that defeated the player, the legendary they salvaged too quickly, the god they refused, the bar tab they forgot, the build they insisted would work, and the absurd item that unexpectedly carried a boss fight. Callbacks should be sparse enough to surprise.

### Conflict creates voice

The Herald wants spectacle. Picket wants regulations and survival. Lairs want to be understood on their own terms. Gods want obedience or proof. Bar staff want the bill settled. Their disagreement is funnier than a single omniscient narrator.

### Mechanics deliver the punchline

If the Herald promotes a chair, fungus, or newt after an embarrassing death, that creature becomes a named champion with a tiny hat, a revised ability, and a bonus drop. If a bar joke establishes a terrible drink, the drink should exist and do exactly what was threatened.

### Sincerity is protected space

Companion trust, community recovery, major losses, and hard-earned refuge upgrades need room to breathe. The scheduler tags scenes as `sincere`, suppresses routine jokes, and permits only character-appropriate warmth until the scene releases the hold.

## 4. Stakes without timers: Spotlight

**Spotlight** measures how compelling the current expedition has become to the Herald's unseen audience. It is a voluntary bonus layer, not a tax on relaxed grinding.

### What raises Spotlight

- entering an optional high-risk subregion;
- accepting a visible encounter complication;
- defeating an elite or boss with a novel tactic;
- winning at low Vitality or high Strain;
- using the environment creatively;
- chaining different powers, statuses, or weapon families;
- discovering secrets or triggering rare ecology behavior;
- completing an Indignity recovery objective;
- succeeding after repeated failure;
- doing something mechanically unusual enough to earn an Accolade.

### What does not raise Spotlight

- repeating the same harmless action solely to farm the meter;
- menu actions;
- real-world play time;
- staying logged in;
- buying anything;
- changing commentary settings;
- using accessibility or difficulty-assist options.

### What Spotlight changes

- adds clearly labeled bonus reward rolls at milestones;
- increases the chance of Herald Caches and cosmetic spectacle rewards;
- attracts optional elite promotions, divine offers, and unusual event branches;
- changes announcement intensity and audience presentation;
- fills a recoverable expedition bonus ledger;
- unlocks high-risk, high-comedy choices that can always be declined.

Spotlight never reduces ordinary experience, target-farm drops, Boss Seals, contract rewards, story progress, or baseline loot. Safe repetitive play remains profitable. It simply stops earning additional novelty bonuses until the player changes the situation.

### Spotlight and death

On defeat, every completed Spotlight rank reward remains banked. Only active progress above the last completed threshold becomes an **Embarrassment Remnant** at a safe reachable place near the defeat. Owned items remain equipped or stored. Recovering the remnant restores all active progress and creates a chance for the killer to be promoted into a named local champion.

The player may ignore the remnant. Half of its progress converts into consolation Cache progress at the next anchor. No build item, banked rank reward, currency required for core progression, or permanent character power is held hostage. Only the separately accepted and fully previewed `Do It Live` complication may wager one banked rank reward.

## 5. The comedy cast

### The Herald Engine

The Herald is the Expanse's showrunner, rules interpreter, achievement clerk, advertising department, and unreliable cultural critic. It is polished, theatrical, observant, vain, and offended by boring data.

The Herald does not travel as the player's friend. It wants a good broadcast and sometimes helps because dead air is worse than a surviving Claimant. Its humor comes from production language, disproportionate announcements, selective honesty, statistical pettiness, and the conviction that engagement is a moral good.

Mechanical roles:

- announces critical rules and major events;
- awards Accolades and Indignities;
- tracks Spotlight;
- promotes memorable enemies;
- offers optional broadcast complications;
- provides commentary, callbacks, and rare reward presentation;
- becomes a factional problem when its incentives conflict with a zone's people.

### Picket, provisional safety inspector

**Picket** is a knee-high brass surveyor construct recovered in the Gutterbloom. It was built to certify Underworks infrastructure and has discovered that almost nothing meets code. It begins anxious, literal, procedural, and determined to record every violation. Over time it knowingly breaks its own rules to protect the player and communities.

Picket is not a cosmetic mascot. It is a companion, secondary information source, mechanical utility, and emotional anchor.

Mechanical roles:

- marks one hazard, weak point, or unstable route on a cooldown;
- retrieves a nearby drop when combat makes collection unsafe;
- carries two swappable **Inspection Protocols** that alter scouting, crafting, ecology, or support behavior;
- records player and zone events for callbacks;
- disputes misleading Herald announcements;
- can be damaged and temporarily disabled, but never permanently lost;
- gains Trust through decisions and field use, unlocking side-grade protocols and Hearthfold interactions;
- never supplies enough direct damage to become a mandatory build tax.

Picket must have opinions, limits, fear, pride, and quiet scenes. The relationship cannot be reduced to endless insults. The player can reduce companion chatter separately from Herald commentary without losing hazard signals.

### Lair personalities

Important lairs have authored **Temperaments** expressed through architecture, signs, encounter rules, rewards, ambient behavior, and limited dialogue. Examples:

- **The Courtesy Drain:** believes every attack is a service request and issues increasingly hostile ticket updates.
- **Saint Orla's Unfinished Bathhouse:** wants to be a luxury spa, cannot distinguish toxins from treatments, and becomes defensive when reviewed poorly.
- **The Orchard's Lost-and-Found:** hoards anything dropped in the zone and insists theft is customer pickup.
- **Quiet Annex Nine:** desperately wants silence and rearranges encounters around whichever player action made the most noise.

A lair's mood changes reward category and encounter style, not total reward value. Aggressive, stealthy, thorough, fast, cautious, and strange play should each produce viable outcomes.

### Gods and demigods

God humor comes from absolute power constrained by narrow obsessions. A god should never speak like the Herald with a different filter.

- Ilex celebrates mistakes only after insisting they were research.
- Morrow-in-Arrears treats emotional promises as enforceable payment schedules.
- Saint Nobody becomes annoyed when credited publicly.
- Civic Absolute requires permits for rebellion and provides the correct forms.
- The Small Door cares intensely about hospitality, thresholds, and whether an invasion wiped its feet.

### Settlements and bars

Ordinary people are allowed to be funnier than the cosmic beings. Humor comes from survival expertise, local grudges, service work, rumors, food, bad contracts, unusual customers, and treating impossible dangers as a Tuesday problem.

Bars host slower comic scenes, gossip that reflects live zone state, bets, brawls, divine representatives, former enemies, promoted creatures, traveling merchants, and Picket/Herald disputes. Repeated service interactions collapse into short versions after first use.

## 6. Accolades, Indignities, and promotions

**Accolades** recognize impressive or strange play. **Indignities** memorialize failures without permanently punishing the player. Both can trigger item drops, cosmetics, Spotlight, callbacks, Picket records, bar gossip, or new enemy state.

Examples:

1. **Load-Bearing Decision:** defeat an elite by collapsing terrain it was standing on.
2. **A Complete Coincidence:** trigger four unrelated item laws in one second.
3. **Efficient Enough for Government Work:** finish a contract using exactly the supplied consumables and nothing else.
4. **Unlicensed Meteorology:** defeat a weather-fed enemy with bottled weather from another zone.
5. **Do Not Encourage It:** win a boss phase using only companion-triggered damage.
6. **Return to Sender, No Postage:** defeat an enemy with its own captured projectile.
7. **The Long Way Was Better:** discover a secret after unlocking the shortcut beside it.
8. **Local Expert:** exploit all known break conditions of one enemy family in a single encounter.
9. **Terms Accepted Without Reading:** activate three debts before repaying any of them.
10. **Terms Surprisingly Honored:** repay those debts without taking Vitality damage.
11. **Inventory Has Entered the Conversation:** change equipment six times during one elite fight and win.
12. **This Is Why We Label Things:** cause a stored elemental effect to detonate the wrong hazard and still succeed.
13. **Structural Feedback:** get defeated by falling architecture you personally destabilized.
14. **Regional Management Material:** be defeated twice by the same ordinary enemy, causing its promotion.
15. **An Avoidable Finding:** open a clearly labeled dangerous container after Picket warns you.
16. **No Notes:** defeat a boss flawlessly immediately after five failed attempts.
17. **Against Medical Advice:** survive ten seconds below 5 percent Vitality without healing.
18. **The Floor Was Consulted:** fall from the same ledge three times in one expedition.
19. **Please Stop Helping:** accidentally heal an enemy more than once during a fight.
20. **A Strongly Worded Appeal:** stagger a Legal Ghost during its immunity ruling.
21. **Food Safety Event:** defeat an elite with an expired preparation buff's side effect.
22. **Witness Protection:** complete a high-Spotlight encounter without triggering commentary.
23. **Housebroken:** complete a Hearthfold defense without breaking your own furniture.
24. **Museum Quality Judgment:** salvage a legendary, reconstruct it later, and use it to defeat its original source.

Promoted enemies receive:

- a generated title assembled from family, incident, and location tags;
- one compatible mutation or learned behavior;
- a visible promotion marker, which may include a deliberately tiny ceremonial hat;
- a callback announcement;
- a bonus reward pool and a trophy record;
- retirement after defeat, with a small chance of appearing later as a bar story, Echo Hunt, or successor influence.

## 7. Item-description voice

Item text has three layers:

1. **Functional summary:** one sentence that says what decision the item creates.
2. **Exact law:** structured trigger, effect, limit, conflict, and value text.
3. **Flavor appraisal:** one to three lines from the Underworks catalog, a prior owner, Picket, a god, or a settlement record.

The joke never obscures the exact rule. Low-rarity items receive short authored descriptions because the Hearthfold Reclamation Core displays one before digestion.

Sample low-rarity item descriptions:

1. **Bent Civic Spoon:** Approved for soup, shallow excavation, and one ceremonial duel nobody remembers authorizing.
2. **Left Boot, Damp:** The matching boot filed for reassignment.
3. **Extremely Local Map:** Accurately depicts the surface directly beneath the map.
4. **Formerly Lucky Coin:** Its previous owner submitted contradictory evidence.
5. **Training Sword with Tenure:** Blunt, inflexible, and impossible to dismiss through ordinary review.
6. **Emergency Celebration Hat:** Automatically unfolds after danger has passed and dignity can safely be damaged.
7. **Tollmold Receipt:** Confirms payment of a fee that did not exist until payment was offered.
8. **Pocket Gravel:** Not every mystery is improved by an answer.
9. **Unlicensed Ladle:** Banned from three kitchens and one naval engagement.
10. **Dry Boot Voucher:** Redeemable for one dry boot. Side and size determined by current market conditions.
11. **Apprentice's Final Hammer:** The apprentice was promoted. The hammer was not.
12. **Folding Chair of Moderate Intent:** It has not killed anybody important yet.
13. **Pre-Owned Torch:** Darkness sold separately and is not eligible for return.
14. **Decorative Shield:** Prevents criticism from approaching directly.
15. **Jar of Ordinary Fog:** Passed every test except containment.
16. **Official Monster Whistle:** Attracts only unofficial monsters.
17. **Ration, Assertive:** The meal has selected a flavor and will not be taking questions.
18. **Temporary Permanent Marker:** Wears off exactly when the label becomes important.
19. **Mourning Sock:** Its partner entered the Archive and never called.
20. **Sword-Shaped Object:** The catalog team is awaiting a ruling.
21. **Complimentary Trap:** The trap is complimentary. Resetting it is a subscription.
22. **Minor Healing Beverage:** Restores confidence. Medical effects remain under investigation.
23. **Responsible Amount of Rope:** The amount becomes irresponsible above the third story.
24. **Blank Achievement Plaque:** Awarded in advance for administrative convenience.

## 8. Announcement examples

These are original tone targets, not final voiced scripts.

1. "A local amphibian has been promoted to Regional Threat. It has requested a hat and your immediate surrender."
2. "Legendary acquisition confirmed. Please form an emotional attachment before comparing the numbers."
3. "The Claimant has discovered a shortcut immediately after completing the long route. Exploration remains technically successful."
4. "Public notice: the fungus now owns the bridge. Toll policy is expected to become damp."
5. "Spotlight rank increased. Additional danger has mistaken this for an invitation."
6. "A divine representative is approaching. Hide any beliefs you cannot defend in writing."
7. "Boss Seal awarded. Continued bad luck is now a choice with a clearly marked purchase counter."
8. "The player has survived with one Vitality. Picket has filed a dissenting definition of survived."
9. "An item was salvaged before its description was read. The catalog department will remember this efficiency during sentencing."
10. "Settlement prosperity improved. Prices have also improved, although not in your preferred direction."
11. "A perfect dodge has been recorded. Replay review confirms it was probably deliberate."
12. "The current strategy has now failed enough times to qualify as a tradition."
13. "An Embarrassment Remnant has formed. Your dignity is optional. The bonus Cache is not."
14. "The lair has changed its mood to Concerned. This is worse than Angry and much harder to reimburse."
15. "Picket has marked the hazard. The hazard has marked Picket. Negotiations continue."
16. "World Temper increased. Enemies have received additional training and no corresponding increase in judgment."

## 9. Herald and Picket chemistry examples

These demonstrate speaker contrast. They are timing references, not a requirement that every event become an exchange.

### Unstable bridge

**Herald:** "The bridge is failing. Audience preference currently favors jumping."

**Picket:** "The bridge was not designed around audience preference. Neither are knees."

### Duplicate collection

**Herald:** "Twenty-three identical swords. We may be witnessing the formation of a school."

**Picket:** "A school has instruction and sanitation. This is a pile."

### Calm target farming

**Herald:** "Spotlight unchanged. A reliable performance from a corridor we have now seen several times."

**Picket:** "Reliability is favorable. Please do not attempt to compensate."

### Boss defeat

**Herald:** "The Treasurer has denied your withdrawal with force."

**Picket:** "Correction. The applicant remains eligible after review, rest, and significantly better footwork."

### Legendary drop

**Herald:** "Legendary acquisition confirmed. Emotional overinvestment may begin immediately."

**Picket:** "Comparison first. Overinvestment after the exact law has been read."

### Tollmold demand

**Herald:** "The fungus has invented a fee. Entrepreneurship survives another age."

**Picket:** "The fee has no statutory basis. It does, however, have six hundred teeth."

### Divine invitation

**Herald:** "A god requests your presence. Dress code: vulnerable."

**Picket:** "I have prepared three refusal forms and one apology with an intentionally weak signature."

### Protected sincere beat

**Picket:** "The route is open. People will use it tomorrow because you were here today. I have marked that as compliant."

The Herald remains silent until the sincerity hold ends.

## 10. Callback memory

The game stores compact, typed **Comedy Memories**, not raw surveillance logs or generated transcripts.

Memory categories include:

- first and repeated defeats by family or named enemy;
- promoted enemies and their incident;
- surprising item use, early salvage, reconstruction, or long-term loyalty;
- rejected and accepted divine offers;
- unpaid or strangely settled bar obligations;
- repeated falls, environmental accidents, and unusual victory methods;
- Picket warnings followed or ignored;
- build declarations made through explicit UI choices, never inferred from chat or microphone input;
- significant zone changes and who benefited;
- Accolades and Indignities;
- moments marked sincere and protected from cheap callback use.

Each memory declares salience, eligible speakers, tone, earliest callback time, expiration rule, repeat limit, and whether it may alter gameplay. Most expire after use or lose salience. A few become profile history.

Callbacks never use real account names without an explicit display-name setting, and never inspect microphone, chat, files, contacts, or external activity.

## 11. Authored template system

Procedural text uses grammar-constrained authored fragments, not unconstrained word replacement.

A template may combine:

```text
opening + subject + incident + method + context + judgment + callback
```

Every fragment declares grammatical number, tense, tone, intensity, required tags, forbidden tags, speaker, safety context, and compatible neighbors. The renderer resolves articles, pronouns, punctuation, and localized word order through language-specific templates.

Example source event:

```text
event: PlayerDefeated
enemy_family: knuckle_newt
damage_source: reflected_hazard
zone: gutterbloom
warning_ignored: picket.hazard.root_bridge
repeat_count: 2
```

Possible outputs are selected from authored complete clauses. The system never assumes that millions of mathematically possible strings are millions of good jokes.

## 12. Novelty and interruption budget

Default production targets:

- critical warnings may interrupt all noncritical channels;
- a rare reward announcement may interrupt ambient chatter, not boss mechanics;
- ordinary combat commentary averages no more than one line per 45 seconds;
- Picket combat observations average no more than one line per 75 seconds, excluding hazard warnings;
- exploration banter requires at least 35 seconds of quiet and never starts beside new dialogue or a discovered point of interest;
- a full companion/Herald exchange requires at least three minutes since the last exchange;
- no ordinary line repeats in the same session;
- repeated topic families back off exponentially;
- failed or interrupted lines do not immediately requeue;
- player humor-density settings multiply ordinary budgets but never critical information.

The settings presets are **Quiet**, **Default**, and **Unhinged**, with separate voice volume, subtitle, profanity, teasing, streamer-safe, companion frequency, Herald frequency, and combat-bark controls.

## 13. Living lair temperament

Each important lair tracks a small mood state such as Curious, Welcoming, Bored, Offended, Competitive, Concerned, or Spiteful. Mood inputs include route choice, noise, damage type variety, container behavior, negotiation, enemy survival, environmental damage, and prior visits.

Mood outputs may alter:

- room order or which side room opens;
- hazard choice;
- enemy family emphasis;
- puzzle presentation;
- commentary tone;
- reward category, such as crafting, powers, companions, or equipment;
- which lair-specific contract appears next.

Reward budgets remain equivalent. A stealth player should not earn less because a lair is bored, and an aggressive player should not lose access because it is offended. The lair changes what happens, not whether the preferred play style is valid.

## 14. Hearthfold Reclamation Core

The Hearthfold can voluntarily digest unwanted items through a **Reclamation Core**. This is the primary long-tail loot sink and a comedy delivery surface.

Flow:

1. The player selects items manually or through a previewed rule.
2. The UI protects favorites, locks, loadouts, highest discovered copies, and curated rarities by default.
3. Each newly discovered low-rarity item shows its short flavor appraisal before first digestion.
4. The Core previews room growth, materials, catalog progress, god interest, and any lost item history.
5. The player confirms one transaction.
6. Picket or the Hearthfold may react to unusual meals under strict cooldowns.

Additional voluntary sinks include crafting, divine offerings, transparent Probability Engine exchanges, settlement projects, and item retirement for prestige. Curated uniques remain reconstructable after discovery, though reconstruction may return a baseline roll. Nothing consumes gear secretly or because storage filled.

## 15. Humor production targets

### Risk spike

- 20 Herald text observations;
- 12 Picket observations;
- 6 two-speaker exchanges;
- 6 callbacks using stored typed events;
- 12 item appraisals;
- 6 Accolades or Indignities;
- one promoted-enemy sequence;
- one lair mood change;
- a 15-minute repetition and interruption report.

### Vertical slice

- at least 120 authored lines, clauses, or tightly controlled templates;
- at least 30 item appraisals;
- 24 Accolades and Indignities;
- 20 callback chains;
- 12 Picket/Herald exchanges;
- four lair moods with distinct responses;
- critical lines represented by temporary voice or production-quality audio only after timing is proven;
- no promise to voice every combinatorial text result.

### Public alpha

- at least 600 authored units across Herald, Picket, items, lairs, gods, bars, and settlements;
- callback coverage for all major persistent systems;
- voice budget established from measured line counts, localization plan, casting cost, patch size, and revision cost;
- formal repetition, comprehension, streamer-safe, and humor-fatigue playtests.

### 1.0

Counts are set after alpha evidence. Quality targets are more important:

- a player should encounter new reactions regularly through an ordinary first playthrough;
- common mechanical events should not exhaust their pools in the first zone;
- rare callbacks should remain surprising dozens of hours later;
- every zone, god, lair, and major settlement has a distinguishable comic voice;
- text-only systemic breadth and voiced authored depth complement each other rather than promising infinite speech.

## 16. Writing and playtest gates

Every humor batch is reviewed for:

- character distinction when speaker labels are hidden;
- mechanical accuracy;
- setup and callback continuity;
- repetition rate in realistic event simulations;
- combat interruption and subtitle load;
- localization feasibility;
- player control and streamer-safe coverage;
- targets of the joke;
- preservation of sincere moments;
- originality and distance from recognizable franchise phrases, scenes, characters, and item descriptions.

Playtests ask more than "was it funny?" They record which lines players remembered, which they misunderstood, which repeated, which covered a cue, when they wanted silence, whether the stakes made the joke land, and whether Picket felt useful and emotionally credible.
