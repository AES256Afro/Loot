# Product Vision

## One-sentence pitch

Explore an endless network of living fantasy-industrial zones, turn bizarre loot into increasingly unfair character builds, be judged by a theatrical world intelligence, and carry an upgradeable pocket sanctuary from frontier to frontier at your own pace.

## The promise

The game should make the player think, "I will do one more route because I know what I am hunting, but something strange may happen on the way."

It is a long-session and long-tail grinding game. The world offers direction but does not rush the player. Progress persists. Old zones remain useful. Rare items create new builds instead of merely adding a larger number.

## Original identity

The setting is **The Living Expanse**, an impossible patchwork world grown around an ancient infrastructure called the **Underworks**. The Underworks rearranges boundaries, fabricates challenges, resurrects local ecosystems, and broadcasts noteworthy events through an intelligence called the **Herald Engine**.

Long ago, the machinery was built to preserve worlds. It has since confused preservation, competition, tourism, religion, and maintenance. Gods bargain for influence inside it. Cities exploit it. Monsters adapt to it. The Herald Engine tries to turn everything into good programming.

This premise supports comedy, commentary, divine interference, living environments, bizarre rewards, and dramatic announcements without copying another franchise's characters or terminology.

## Inspiration translated into original systems

The goal is to preserve the desired categories of fun while creating our own expression and removing the pressure mechanics the user dislikes.

| Desired element | Original implementation |
| --- | --- |
| A world intelligence that observes contestants | The Herald Engine observes Claimants through an authored, configurable commentary scheduler. |
| A dungeon authority able to change rules | The Underworks maintains zones, anchors, ecology, rewards, and local rule anomalies. |
| Distinct floors with radically different identities | Persistent zones have their own ecology, settlements, traversal, factions, hazards, bosses, and state cycles. |
| Absurd achievement rewards and loot boxes | Feats award earned Caches with visible pools, instant opening, target attunement, and bad-luck protection. |
| Huge class lists and surprising choices | Unlockable Disciplines, Advanced Paths, Origins, collectible powers, and item laws create reversible build combinations. |
| Safe rooms between dangers | The Hearthfold is a persistent pocket refuge with rooms, staff, crafting, storage, training, visitors, and routing upgrades. |
| Gods and powerful sponsors | Original gods offer covenants, proofs, persistent Recognition, non-destructive Tension, relics, and competing philosophies. |
| Showmanship and audience reaction | Spotlight rewards unusual play with bonus spectacle and rewards, but never reduces baseline farming or basic access. |
| Bizarre, build-defining items | Legendary laws alter triggers, resources, movement, statuses, companions, projectiles, and zone interactions. |
| Constant environmental surprises | Living-zone state and event directors change routes, populations, resources, patrols, weather, and boss succession. |

## Deliberate changes from a pressure-driven floor crawler

- Floors become large revisitable zones.
- Global survival countdowns are removed.
- The player is never expelled from a zone because real time elapsed.
- Advancing does not lock previous zones.
- New content does not require abandoning the current character.
- Progress does not reset for a season.
- Rare gear is never dropped on death.
- Storage does not depend on tiny bags or destructive cleanup.
- Build experimentation does not require a new save.
- Classes are unlockable and swappable in safe spaces.
- Failed world events create recovery play instead of permanent missed content.
- Bosses have nearby checkpoints and direct-purchase protection for repeat drops.
- Difficulty timers are optional scoring mutators, not access rules.
- Commentary can be tuned or muted without losing combat warnings.
- World changes are consequential but preserve access to critical rewards.
- Reward Caches are earned, transparent, instantly opened, and never monetized through random purchases.
- Old zones receive new state combinations, successors, contracts, and target rewards rather than becoming obsolete.
- Long-tail progression adds breadth, mastery, collections, cosmetics, and difficulty options instead of unlimited base-stat inflation.

## Audience

- Players who enjoy Diablo-style build experimentation and loot hunting.
- Players who enjoy revisiting broad MMO-like zones but prefer solo-friendly, non-expiring progress.
- Players who like systemic comedy and reactive commentary.
- Collectors who want searchable, expandable storage and completion goals.
- Players who want demanding combat without extraction loss or schedule pressure.

## Seven pillars

### 1. Zones, not disposable floors

Each major zone is a persistent region with a visual identity, ecology, settlements, routes, local factions, public events, lairs, secrets, resources, and a state that changes over time. Zones are not deleted when the player advances. Later systems create reasons to return.

### 2. Loot changes play

Common gear establishes reliable stat foundations. Affixes support targeted builds. Uniques bend rules. Legendaries create or transform mechanics. Mythic Relics can redefine an entire loadout but have hard tradeoffs. The best item is often situational, not automatically the newest.

### 3. The grind respects the player

The player can target rewards, repeat content, pause freely, respec in safe spaces, retain discoveries, and recover from death without losing treasured gear. Drop protection and duplicate conversion prevent infinite bad luck.

### 4. A world that reacts and remembers

Zones track ecology, faction control, threat, weather, resource pressure, local prosperity, completed events, boss succession, and player notoriety. Changes open and close opportunities but do not permanently erase critical content.

### 5. Humor remembers and changes things

The Herald Engine, companion Picket, gods, lairs, item appraisals, bars, Accolades, and Indignities notice builds, failures, streaks, strange tactics, discoveries, social choices, and improbable loot. A joke can promote an enemy, change a lair mood, open a recovery objective, or return as a callback hours later. Humor must never cover important combat audio or repeat into annoyance.

### 6. Home travels with you

The Hearthfold is a pocket refuge attached to stable anchors. Its rooms, staff, crafting stations, trophies, storage, defenses, and visitors persist across zones. It is both a functional progression system and the player's emotional home.

### 7. Power without permanent regret

Build commitment should create identity during an expedition, not punish experimentation for an entire save. Players may maintain loadouts, store gear freely, refund discipline points in the Hearthfold, and replay earlier encounters with new combinations.

## The core loop

1. **Prepare:** choose a discipline loadout, equipment, powers, consumables, target loot, and a zone contract in the Hearthfold or a settlement.
2. **Explore:** travel through a zone, gather resources, find routes, meet factions, solve local events, and discover lairs.
3. **Fight:** use readable action combat against ecological enemy groups, elites, champions, and bosses.
4. **Get paid:** receive equipment, materials, powers, Caches, recipes, reputation, trophies, and Spotlight bonuses.
5. **Adapt:** equip a drop immediately, bank it remotely at limited field terminals, or continue with an expedition modifier.
6. **Change the zone:** victories and choices alter control, hazards, routes, population, and available events.
7. **Return and improve:** expand the Hearthfold, craft, target the next reward, adjust the build, and head out again.

The smallest satisfying loop should take 5 to 8 minutes. A lair run should take 15 to 25 minutes. A zone arc should provide 8 to 15 hours of first-time discovery and much longer mastery play.

## Stakes without deadlines

Spotlight provides tension during an expedition without imposing a countdown. Novel, risky, skillful, or spectacular actions raise Spotlight. Milestones add bonus reward rolls, Herald Caches, optional enemy promotions, divine attention, and strange event choices.

Repeated safe grinding still grants full baseline experience, target drops, contracts, Boss Seals, and ordinary loot. It simply stops adding novelty bonuses until the situation changes. Spotlight never decays because the player pauses, leaves the game, plays slowly, uses accessibility features, or declines a challenge.

On defeat, every completed Spotlight rank reward remains banked. Only unfinished progress toward the next rank becomes an optional Embarrassment Remnant. The player keeps every owned item. Recovering the remnant restores that active progress and may turn the killer into a named promoted enemy with extra loot. Ignoring it converts half of the progress into consolation Cache progress instead of blocking play.

## Anti-frustration constitution

These are product requirements, not optional polish:

- No mandatory timers on zones, bosses, quests, or loot claims.
- No authoritative world, NPC, relationship, or situation advancement while the game is closed.
- No named character, essential service, or unique reward disappears through an unseen automatic outcome.
- No real-world NPC appointments, event deadlines, weekend modifiers, or login-sensitive opportunities.
- No seasonal deletion of characters, storage, story access, or earned power.
- No real-money randomized rewards.
- No loss of equipped or stored gear on death.
- No durability or repair chores on equipped gear.
- No tiny bag forcing town trips every few minutes.
- No unskippable announcer speeches; commentary has volume, frequency, subtitle, history, and mute controls.
- No account, remote commentary model, graph server, or persistent online service required for solo play.
- No permanently missable build-defining items.
- No class choice that requires restarting the whole game to test another play style.
- No enemy scaling that makes every improvement feel neutralized.
- No forced multiplayer for core progression.
- No rare-drop system without target farming, duplicate conversion, and bad-luck protection.
- No content vaulting. Old zones receive rotating reasons to revisit them.
- No boss run that requires repeating a long empty approach after every defeat.
- No inventory clutter without search, filters, tags, comparison, favorites, loadouts, and configurable auto-salvage.
- No penalty for reducing commentary, companion chatter, profanity, teasing, screen effects, combat speed, or other supported accessibility settings.

## Difficulty model

Difficulty comes from optional pressure, not a universal countdown.

- Each zone has a recommended power band and several higher **World Temper** tiers.
- Players can increase enemy tactics, density, elite mutations, hazard complexity, and boss phases.
- Higher tiers improve quantity, affix odds, targeted currencies, and cosmetic prestige, but do not lock the entire story.
- Optional challenges may use time as a scoring dimension, never as the default rule for being allowed to remain in a zone.
- Death returns the player to the nearest discovered anchor, restores ordinary enemies according to ecology rules, and reduces unclaimed Spotlight. Items and permanent progress remain.

## Scope boundaries

### Required for the first public alpha

- One polished zone plus one smaller hub region.
- A complete combat, loot, inventory, save, Hearthfold, Spotlight, Picket, humor/commentary, and living-zone loop.
- At least four viable build families.
- At least 60 curated items, including 20 build-changing uniques or legendaries.
- At least six enemy families, 12 elite mutations, two lairs, and three bosses.

### Desired for 1.0

- Eight major zones and four hub cities.
- 20 settlements or semi-safe social spaces.
- 12 base disciplines with cross-discipline builds and advanced paths.
- 24 weapon families, 10 armor families, 250 affixes, 200 powers, and 120 curated unique or legendary items.
- At least 120 enemy archetypes, 50 bosses or named champions, and 80 authored world events.
- An eight-room Hearthfold with staff, visitors, trophies, Reclamation, crafting, training, research, and defense events.
- A complete offline single-player experience.

### Explicitly deferred

- MMO-scale shared world.
- PvP.
- player trading or an auction house;
- user-generated cash economy;
- fully generative story or unbounded AI dialogue;
- mobile and console ports before PC input, performance, and accessibility are stable;
- co-op before a technical spike proves that it will not compromise saves, simulation, or solo play.

## Sustainable massive-content strategy

"Massive" comes from three layers working together:

1. **Curated foundations:** distinctive weapon families, enemy families, zones, powers, bosses, and legendary items.
2. **Validated combinations:** materials, quality tiers, affixes, elite mutations, environmental states, and event parameters combine safely.
3. **Authored exceptions:** the most memorable rewards and encounters deliberately break selected rules.

Raw permutation count is not a quality metric. Every generated result must remain readable, legal for its slot, useful to at least one build, and explainable in the item comparison UI.
