# Initial Content Catalog

This is a design inventory, not implemented content. Names and numbers are working material. The purpose is to prove that the original setting can support a large, coherent content library before production multiplies it.

## 2. Zone atlas

### Z01: The Gutterbloom

A broken trade city's storm drains have become a luminous fungal wetland. Merchant roofs form islands above spore canals; root bridges grow or collapse based on moisture and faction activity. The hub is **Latchmarket**, and the semi-safe bar is **The Dry Boot**. Enemies include Knuckle Newts, Tollmold colonies, and Sump Knights. If the player defeats the Rain Treasurer, water levels fall, a smuggler route opens, and fire-based predators move in.

### Z02: The Brass Orchard

Endless copper trees grow mechanical fruit tended by territorial pollinator constructs. The city of **Pip Foundry** argues over whether the orchard is a farm, a machine, or a god. Harvest pressure changes enemy behavior and available crafting metals. Boss succession alternates between a caretaker intelligence and enormous feral harvesters.

### Z03: The Glass Tidelands

A coast where each tide hardens into transparent ridges before the next arrives. Creatures swim through solid glass as if it were water. **Low Prism** migrates on sled foundations, while **The Cracked Cup** opens only at low resonance. Storms reveal buried ships, reflect projectiles, and create temporary mirror predators.

### Z04: Choir of Ash

A volcanic basin where soot remembers voices and repeats them as dangerous weather. Villages communicate through tuned bells to avoid feeding the ash. The player can quiet, redirect, or weaponize remembered sound. The demi-god Cantor Below changes form based on which songs the zone has absorbed.

### Z05: Clockfen

A peat marsh growing around failed timekeeping machinery. Different pools run at different local speeds, but the player is never subjected to a real-world deadline. **Morrow-on-Stilts** sells preserved moments as crafting ingredients. Ecology events include accelerated hatching, reverse decay, and patrols trapped in repeating routes.

### Z06: Skygrave Reach

Floating tomb-islands circle a permanent updraft. Rope towns cling to moving landmasses, and winged scavengers nest inside colossal burial engines. Route geometry changes as islands orbit. Players gain traversal tools that turn old vertical routes into new hunting circuits.

### Z07: The Pale Rail

An immense train line crosses a moonlit wasteland, but many stations have grown into stationary towns while the tracks keep wandering. Trains are moving dungeons, traders, and evacuation routes. Track ownership affects prices, patrols, and which mobile lairs dock at each station.

### Z08: The Sunken Parliament

A drowned civic complex inhabited by argumentative amphibian houses, legal ghosts, and predatory paperwork. Air pockets serve as settlements. Laws passed by local factions become temporary zone modifiers, and the player can campaign, sabotage, or exploit loopholes without being forced into a single ending.

### Z09: Candlewild

A dark forest illuminated by candles growing from antlered trees. Light attracts helpful guides and hungry mimics. **Waxhome** melts and rebuilds in different clearings. The zone tracks how much darkness the player has disturbed, changing paths and predator activity.

### Z10: The Bone-Rain Steppe

Storms drop fossil fragments that assemble into temporary animals. Nomad workshops chase the most valuable fronts. Bone density changes crafting, cover, and which extinct monsters can reform. The local bar, **Weatherproof**, is built inside a walking shell.

### Z11: Salt Mirror Basin

A white desert reflects possible versions of travelers. Reflections can help, duel, steal, or become recruitable echoes. Settlements use fabric roofs and deliberately imperfect architecture to avoid replication. The zone's living state tracks unresolved duplicates and the stability of mirrored routes.

### Z12: The Velvet Maw

A vast living cavern lined with soft tissue, jewel-like nerves, and domesticated immune creatures. Settlers disagree over whether they live inside a beast, a world, or a divine machine. Healing is powerful here, but repeated wounds teach the ecology to counter the player's favorite damage types.

### Z13: Last Festival District

A city-sized celebration whose automated hosts refuse to admit the guests left centuries ago. Parades become moving encounters, prizes become loot pools, and faction control determines whether each district is joyous, predatory, mournful, or revolutionary. The event schedule advances with play, not real-world time.

### Z14: The Starved Library

Books consume memories, spells, and occasionally each other. Librarian factions cultivate genres as biomes. Reading can reveal recipes and powers, but overfeeding one collection changes local enemies and architecture. The boss is whichever subject has become too dominant.

### Z15: Red Weather March

A frontier where storms fall sideways and paint matter into temporary life. Forts use color-draining towers to hold safe ground. Wind direction, pigment density, and faction infrastructure alter routes and enemy populations. Players can bottle weather for powers and crafting.

### Z16: The White Furnace

A late-game polar foundry that burns cold and freezes heat. Gods send envoys to contest its ability to manufacture divine vessels. The zone alternates between production, revolt, and shutdown states, each with different bosses and item laws.

## 3. Origins

Origins give a modest passive and an exploration utility. None lock classes or gear.

| Origin | Passive | Utility |
| --- | --- | --- |

| Foundling of Brass | Constructs reveal one resistance after scanning. | Repair simple field mechanisms. |
| Echo Double | First use of a copied power costs less Drive. | Question reflections and memory echoes. |

## 4. Disciplines and Advanced Paths

### D01: Riftblade

A mobile weapon fighter who cuts short-lived seams through space. Its signature **Afterline** repeats part of the most recent melee attack from a stored position.

- **Doorless Duelist:** precision teleports, counters, and single-target execution.
- **Crowd Cutter:** wide seams, chained marks, and movement through groups.

### D02: Cinderwright

A heat engineer who builds Pressure through fire and vents it through weapons, traps, or armor.

- **Boiler Saint:** converts heat into Guard and retaliatory steam.
- **Kiln Tyrant:** escalates Burning, explosions, and hazardous terrain.

### D03: Grave Gardener

A Rot and companion controller who plants remains that grow into temporary organisms.

- **Bloom Shepherd:** cultivates durable allied creatures and healing growth.
- **Carrion Orchardist:** consumes summons for poison, detonations, and resource loops.

### D04: Storm Courier

A speed discipline that delivers charged marks between enemies, allies, and terrain nodes.

- **Thunder Post:** ranged chains, marked routes, and projectile delivery.
- **Last-Mile Runner:** dodge attacks, speed conversion, and close-range bursts.

### D05: Chorus Binder

A resonance caster who records combat sounds and replays them as shields, attacks, or commands.

- **War Cantor:** stacks rhythmic attacks and team-style auras.
- **Hush Architect:** silence zones, delayed echoes, and enemy interruption.

### D06: Beast Broker

A monster specialist who negotiates with, studies, and temporarily contracts zone creatures.

- **Pack Notary:** multiple lesser companions and shared triggers.
- **Apex Advocate:** one powerful contracted beast with equipable traits.

### D07: Lantern Savant

A Radiance and Gloom manipulator who moves light sources to expose weaknesses and create safe ground.

- **Noonkeeper:** beams, reveals, healing light, and anti-stealth.
- **Night Collector:** shadow movement, blind zones, and stored darkness.

### D08: Debt Saint

A risk discipline that borrows health, cooldown, currency, or future Strain for power now, then repays through skilled actions.

- **Mercy Auditor:** turns repayment into healing, protection, and ally benefits.
- **Red Collector:** compounds debt into burst damage and executes defaulting enemies.

### D09: Scrap Oracle

A construct builder who predicts short combat branches and assembles disposable machines from salvage.

- **Probability Mechanic:** traps, decoys, crit manipulation, and forecast bonuses.
- **Junk Monarch:** turrets, drones, and battlefield salvage loops.

### D10: Veil Strider

A misdirection discipline that leaves false positions, swaps with decoys, and exploits enemies that commit to the wrong target.

- **False Witness:** clones, threat control, and reflected projectiles.
- **Backstage Knife:** ambushes, stealth windows, and marked executions.

### D11: Feastkeeper

A preparation discipline that turns gathered ingredients and defeated enemy traits into temporary combat courses.

- **Battle Chef:** deployable meals, team buffs, and weaponized cookware.
- **Hunger Engine:** consumes statuses and enemy traits for escalating solo power.

### D12: Threshold Warden

A space-control tank who places doors, barriers, and linked thresholds.

- **House Guard:** protection zones, interception, and fortified revival.
- **Trespass Judge:** traps enemies between thresholds and punishes movement.

## 5. Initial power library

### Rift and weapon powers

1. **Step Between:** short teleport that stores an Afterline at the departure point.
2. **Return Stroke:** the Afterline repeats your last heavy attack at reduced force.
3. **Seam Rake:** send three spatial cuts across the ground.
4. **Borrowed Reach:** the next melee attack gains extreme range but adds Strain.
5. **Cross Examination:** counter an incoming melee attack and mark the attacker Exposed.
6. **Open Secret:** create a doorway through thin cover for attacks and movement.

### Heat and Cinder powers

7. **Boiler Skin:** convert current Pressure into Guard and steam damage.
8. **Kiln Mouth:** channel a cone of heat that intensifies while held.
9. **Redline Rivet:** fire a spike that detonates when its target becomes Burning.
10. **Cold Vent:** clear Pressure, inflict Brittle nearby, and slow your next power.
11. **Foundry Step:** leave a molten footprint after each dodge for a short duration.
12. **Overdue Explosion:** place a bomb whose damage grows each time you delay detonation.

### Rot and growth powers

13. **Corpsebud:** grow a biting plant from a defeated enemy.
14. **Friendly Mold:** coat an ally or summon in spores that heal on status application.
15. **Root Receipt:** tether an enemy to the ground and gain materials if it dies tethered.
16. **Rot Dividend:** consume all Rot buildup for Drive and a spreading cloud.
17. **Orchard March:** command all active growths to uproot and follow.
18. **Second Harvest:** regrow the last expired summon at half duration.

### Storm and movement powers

19. **Signed Lightning:** throw a charged mark that chains when you cross its path.
20. **Express Route:** sprint along a projected line, damaging crossed enemies.
21. **Return to Sender:** catch one projectile during a dodge and fire it back as Storm damage.
22. **Static Address:** create a node that pulls chain lightning toward it.
23. **Priority Delivery:** teleport the most recent mark to a new target and trigger it.
24. **Weather Delay:** suspend airborne projectiles, then release them together.

### Sound and control powers

25. **Keep the Beat:** attacks on rhythm build a stacking damage and Guard bonus.
26. **Hush Room:** create a silence field that suppresses enemy casting and Herald banter.
27. **Recorded Impact:** store the next loud hit and replay it at a chosen location.
28. **Refrain:** repeat the last non-signature power at reduced strength and cost.
29. **Bad Note:** detune a target so its next special attack staggers itself.
30. **Audience of One:** focus all aura effects onto one target at multiplied strength.

### Beast and companion powers

31. **Temporary Employment:** recruit a weakened ordinary beast until it is defeated or dismissed.
32. **Shared Teeth:** companion critical hits empower the player's next melee attack.
33. **Apex Clause:** enlarge one companion and copy an elite mutation onto it temporarily.
34. **Recall Whistle:** pull companions to the player and grant brief immunity.
35. **Natural Witness:** command a companion to reveal tracks, caches, and nearby ambushes.
36. **Breach of Contract:** dismiss a companion in a powerful area attack based on its traits.

### Light and shadow powers

37. **Carry the Noon:** orbiting lantern exposes weak points and burns Gloom creatures.
38. **Pocket Eclipse:** extinguish local light, empowering dodge and ambush effects.
39. **Long Shadow:** teleport to the end of your shadow and leave a blinding flash.
40. **Witness Beam:** channel light that increases damage taken from all revealed sources.

### Debt and risk powers

43. **Advance Payment:** gain Drive immediately and owe twice that amount over time.
44. **Merciful Terms:** prevent lethal damage, then create a repayment challenge.
45. **Compound Interest:** increase all active debts and multiply their associated bonuses.
46. **Paid in Full:** clear a debt after a perfect dodge or weak-point break.
47. **Foreclose:** consume a marked enemy's remaining status durations as burst damage.

### Construct and threshold powers

49. **Bad Forecast:** display where the target's next major attack will land, then trap that area.
50. **Pocket Turret:** assemble a short-lived turret from Salvage.
51. **Useful Failure:** destroyed constructs refund Drive and drop temporary cover.
52. **Front Door:** place one threshold and return to it on recast.
53. **No Soliciting:** erect a barrier that knocks back the first enemy crossing it.
54. **Room for Error:** create a protected zone that stores a portion of incoming damage until it expires.

### Food, alchemy, and universal powers

55. **Hot Service:** throw a meal that creates a healing and Burning zone depending on who touches it.
56. **Taste Test:** consume one active status and gain a matching elemental buff.
57. **Emergency Pickle:** cleanse control effects, but become Conductive briefly.
58. **Improvised Lid:** parry with cookware and reflect frontal projectiles.

## 7. Equipment slots and armor families

Equipment slots are Head, Body, Arms, Legs, Feet, Main Hand, Off Hand or Two Hand, Charm, Mantle, and two Relic slots.

Armor families include Plate, Mail, Weave, Hide, Ceramic, Fungal, Glass, Bone, Living, and Civic Regalia. A family controls movement and defense tendencies but does not restrict Discipline. Cosmetic appearance can be transmuted after discovery.

Each law below is a functional pitch. Production versions need exact values, compatibility tags, VFX, audio, comparison text, source, and automated interaction tests.

### Mythic Relics

49. **World's Hem:** dodge can cross the boundary between two nearby zone states, briefly summoning enemies and hazards from both. Equipping it increases simulation complexity and is disabled in unsupported spaces.
50. **The Hearth That Walked:** summons a small Hearthfold room into a hostile area once per expedition. Using it raises the local threat to maximum afterward.

## 10. Reward Cache catalog

1. **Field Cache:** two ordinary items and materials from the current zone.
2. **Contract Cache:** one choice among three items matching the contract's shown tags.
3. **Hunter Cache:** rewards from a selected enemy family and increased elite-affix chance.
4. **Armory Cache:** weapon- or armor-family attunement with no consumables.
5. **Power Cache:** one power imprint, Familiarity material, and duplicate conversion protection.
6. **Settlement Cache:** local crafting style, cosmetics, recipes, and civic items.
7. **Bar Tab Box:** semi-random rewards previewed through rumors; may contain a harmless oddity.
8. **Ecology Cache:** beast traits, plants, companion components, and zone research.
9. **Herald Cache:** awarded for unusual feats; contains showy items and commentary-reactive laws.
10. **Divine Reliquary:** covenant-specific choices with visible Favor costs and no paid key.
11. **Boss Coffer:** narrow named pool, guaranteed seal, and rising selection quality on repeats.
12. **Successor Coffer:** rewards tied to the current successor boss and one item from its predecessor's pool.
13. **Temper Chest:** rewards scale with selected difficulty mutators, not completion speed.
14. **Archive Recovery Box:** reconstructs a discovered but salvaged item at a lower roll band.
15. **Impossible Parcel:** extremely rare Cache whose contents come from two normally incompatible categories.
16. **Mercy Cache:** awarded by bad-luck protection and guarantees a missing discovered-pool unique.

## 11. Gods and major powers

1. **Ilex, God of Useful Mistakes:** rewards improvisation, failed crafts, ricochets, and recovery after errors. Repetition simply stops creating additional Recognition.
2. **The White Furnace:** treats identity as a material to be refined. Offers transformation and elemental inversion at the cost of new vulnerabilities.
3. **Morrow-in-Arrears:** god of debts, promises, delayed consequences, and second chances. Makes future obligations visible and mechanically negotiable.
4. **Saint Nobody:** a god created by anonymous acts. Rewards helping without credit and offers powers that redirect recognition to allies, decoys, and constructs.
5. **The Orchard Crown:** distributed machine-divinity of cultivation and controlled harvest. Supports constructs, companions, and resource ecosystems.
6. **Lady Lastlight:** protects endings, evacuations, final charges, and things that should have stopped. Her powers strengthen near defeat without requiring it.
7. **The Red Forecast:** living weather intelligence that values motion and adaptation. Dislikes permanent fortification.
8. **The Gentle Maw:** offers healing, absorption, and biological transformation while asking what the player is willing to feed it.
9. **Civic Absolute:** believes every problem can be solved through rules, permits, and enforcement. Grants barriers and authority with bureaucratic costs.
10. **The Unwritten Guest:** a presence in blank books and unfinished maps. Rewards discovery but makes completed knowledge temporarily uncertain.
11. **Choir Without Breath:** a collective god of repeated sound and communal action. Powers grow through rhythm, echoes, and coordinated triggers.
12. **The Small Door:** god or machine of thresholds, hospitality, trespass, and escape. Deeply involved with the Hearthfold.

## 15. Content-quality rules

Every item, power, enemy, event, and zone entry must pass these questions:

1. What gameplay decision does this create?
2. What tags and systems does it interact with?
3. How does the player learn its rule?
4. Can it combine safely with existing laws and mutations?
5. Does it remain useful or interesting after first discovery?
6. Is its source targetable?
7. Is its presentation original and clearly distinct from source inspirations?
8. What automated validation can catch a broken reference, impossible roll, or illegal combination?

Content does not ship merely because it increases a catalog count.
