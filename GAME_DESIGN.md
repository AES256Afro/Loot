# Game Design

## 1. Player experience

The player is a **Claimant**, one of the rare people able to bind Underworks relics without being rewritten by them. A Claimant explores the Living Expanse, takes contracts, intervenes in local conflicts, hunts impossible gear, attracts divine patrons, and turns a tiny pocket shelter into the Hearthfold.

The game is a hybrid strategic-world party RPG. The party travels through top-down kingdom maps, explores closer settlement maps, and enters first-person procedural dungeons and stopped-time tactical combat. The repeatable spine is travel, discovery, social choice, procedural exploration, combat, loot, collection, party experimentation, zone change, and home improvement.

### Nested loops

**The 30-to-90-second combat loop:** read visible enemy intentions, choose targets and one action for each living party member while time is stopped, exploit a status or environmental opportunity, resolve a short exchange, then revise the next plan from the result.

**The five-to-eight-minute route loop:** leave a safe point with a reward target, traverse one meaningful route, resolve two or three encounters or discoveries, make one build or resource decision, and reach a shortcut, event, lair checkpoint, settlement, or anchor.

**The 20-to-30-minute expedition loop:** select a contract and attunement, build Spotlight if desired, complete a lair or boss route, receive a Cache or targeted reward, cause one visible world reaction, and return to improve the Hearthfold or build.

**The one-to-three-hour session loop:** choose freely among farming, exploration, contracts, ecology, factions, Hearthfold work, bosses, or experimentation. A good session creates at least one memorable mechanical story, but the game never requires a death, a Spotlight rank, or a specific amount of progress before saving and leaving.

**The long-term loop:** master zones, unlock Disciplines and Advanced Paths, perfect unusual builds, expand the Hearthfold, influence gods and factions, hunt successors, complete collections, and revisit changed zones without resetting the character.

## 2. Camera, controls, and feel

- First-person cardinal exploration uses deliberate forward and backward steps plus 90-degree turns. Later settings include animated or instant movement, adjustable field of view, turn speed, camera bob, and motion reduction.
- Mouse/keyboard and controller are supported from the first slice. Every essential combat command is available through both direct shortcuts and focusable interface controls.
- Combat has no aiming reticle, reaction window, or hidden initiative race. Targets and enemy intentions remain visible while the player plans.
- Every party member begins with Strike, a class Power, Guard, and a setup Utility. Equipment, collectible powers, consumables, companions, and zone interactions expand those choices without turning the command screen into an unreadable grid.
- Resolution animation may be stylized, but action order, damage sources, status changes, Guard absorption, defeated units, and environmental effects must remain readable.
- Interaction prompts, item cards, enemy intentions, party status, and commentary occupy stable interface regions and never cover the central dungeon view.

## 3. Combat model

### Round cadence

1. **Read:** enemy intentions, targets, damage ranges, statuses, and relevant room interactions are visible.
2. **Plan:** time is fully stopped. Choose one command and target for each living party member. Plans may be reset freely before commitment.
3. **Commit:** filing the plan is the only point that advances combat time.
4. **Resolve:** party actions and enemy intentions execute in a short deterministic exchange with a readable event log and presentation sequence.
5. **Review:** defeated units are removed, persistent statuses tick, rewards or recovery occur, and the next planning phase begins with no countdown.

### Resources

- **Vitality:** health. A member at zero cannot act until recovered. Full-party defeat returns the expedition to a safe anchor without item loss.
- **Guard:** temporary damage prevention created by commands, class powers, items, or allies. The interface shows how much damage it absorbed.
- **Focus:** a future per-member resource for stronger powers and reactions. It is generated by role-appropriate play and never by real-world waiting.
- **Strain:** accumulated by the strongest powers and relic effects. High Strain creates tactical tradeoffs or changes power behavior, but never deletes equipment or corrupts a save.

### Damage vocabulary

Physical damage is divided into Impact, Edge, and Pierce. Energetic damage is divided into Cinder, Frost, Storm, Rot, Radiance, Gloom, and Aether. Enemies have a short, readable set of resistances and break conditions.

### Status rules

Statuses build toward a threshold rather than applying randomly on every hit. Examples include Burning, Brittle, Conductive, Blighted, Exposed, Staggered, Marked, Silenced, and Displaced. Every status has:

- an application source;
- a visible buildup meter on important targets;
- a duration or consumed trigger;
- a clear interaction with at least two powers or item families;
- a boss rule that preserves usefulness without allowing permanent control.

### Enemy readability

Each enemy family has a planning grammar: intention patterns, preferred targets, defensive rules, status relationships, formation behavior, and an escalation trigger. Elite mutations add one changed rule at a time in early difficulty and may combine at higher World Temper. Important intentions are declared before commitment; uncertainty is a specific authored mechanic, not missing information.

### Boss design

Bosses use mechanics already introduced in the zone, then combine them in an unfamiliar way. A boss checkpoint sits near the arena. Repeated attempts skip nonessential introductions. Bosses provide:

- first-kill guaranteed rewards;
- a visible targeted loot pool;
- a repeat currency redeemable for a selected drop;
- higher-Temper mechanics and cosmetic rewards;
- a changed zone state after decisive victories.

## 4. Build structure

A build is composed of five layers so that class identity and loot experimentation can coexist.

### Origin

An Origin describes how the Claimant first survived exposure to the Underworks. It provides a small passive and an exploration utility. Origins never lock equipment or future disciplines.

### Discipline

A Discipline is the closest equivalent to a class. It provides a signature mechanic, a tree of active/passive nodes, and two starting powers. The player may unlock every base Discipline on one profile and can swap in the Hearthfold or settlements.

### Advanced path

At higher Discipline rank, the player equips one Advanced Path. Paths specialize a mechanic rather than replacing the base class. They are unlocked by play requirements, not irreversible dialogue choices.

### Powers

Powers are collectible abilities. A power can have tags such as Projectile, Movement, Guard, Companion, Trap, Channel, Detonation, Summon, or Zone. Equipment and disciplines alter tags, costs, elements, shapes, and triggers.

### Gear laws

Uniques, Legendaries, and Mythic Relics contain **laws** that modify normal game rules. A law might turn excess healing into orbiting weapons, let dodge pass through projectiles and steal them, or cause a companion to equip one of the player's stored weapons. Every law states its trigger, effect, limits, and conflicts.

## 5. Progression without a treadmill

### Character rank

Character rank unlocks shared stat points, equipment capabilities, and system access. It has a designed cap. Later play expands options rather than endlessly inflating base damage.

### Discipline rank

Each Discipline earns rank while equipped. Rank unlocks tree nodes and Advanced Paths. A catch-up bonus applies to Disciplines far below the player's highest rank.

### Familiarity

Weapon families and powers gain Familiarity through use. Familiarity unlocks side-grade techniques, cosmetic flourishes, crafting insight, and collection milestones. It cannot turn a favorite starter weapon into the mathematically best item forever, but upgrading and transmutation can keep beloved items relevant.

### Zone mastery

Exploration, events, faction work, lairs, bosses, ecology discoveries, and secret routes fill a zone ledger. Milestones reveal targeted farming information, add Hearthfold anchors, and unlock higher World Temper.

### Renown

Account-wide Renown tracks broad accomplishments. It opens quality-of-life features, display trophies, additional loadout slots, and small capped bonuses. Infinite repeat tracks reward cosmetics and choice currencies rather than uncapped power.

### Spotlight

Spotlight is an expedition bonus meter that rises through novelty, risk, skill, discovery, recoveries, Accolades, and strange systemic combinations. It creates stakes for comedy and gives the Herald a mechanical job.

Spotlight has five visible ranks. Each completed rank banks a small guaranteed bonus; the active rank improves optional bonus rolls and can attract promoted enemies, divine offers, or broadcast complications. Ordinary rewards never fall below their published baseline when Spotlight is low. Safe grinding remains valid and profitable.

The novelty scorer recognizes event combinations and recent history rather than raw input rate. Repeating the same trigger stops adding Spotlight but never penalizes the player. Accessibility settings, pause time, offline time, commentary settings, and chosen difficulty assists do not reduce it.

`COMMENTARY_SPOTLIGHT_SPEC.md` defines the authoritative rank thresholds, event matrix, candidate selection, banking, defeat interaction, anti-exploit behavior, and automated tests.

## 6. Loot model

### Rarity

1. **Worn:** simple field gear, salvageable and occasionally useful for challenge runs.
2. **Common:** reliable base items with fixed identity.
3. **Fine:** one affix and better crafting potential.
4. **Rare:** multiple affixes with stronger roll bands.
5. **Exotic:** curated item behavior plus random supporting affixes.
6. **Legendary:** a named item with a build-changing law and curated description.
7. **Mythic Relic:** exceptionally rare, quest- or boss-linked item with a powerful law, meaningful cost, and dedicated acquisition protection.

Color is never the only rarity signal. Names, border shapes, icons, sounds, and text labels remain distinguishable with common color-vision deficiencies.

### Item anatomy

Each item instance may contain:

- immutable content ID and schema version;
- base family, item level band, rarity, quality, material, and manufacturer culture;
- implicit property from the base family;
- zero to four standard affixes;
- zero to two special affixes;
- one curated law for Exotic or better items;
- sockets or upgrade tracks where allowed;
- origin record: zone, source, world seed, date, and notable event;
- player state: favorite, lock, tag, loadout membership, cosmetic state;
- deterministic generation seed so a save can validate or reconstruct it.

### Drop generation

The reward pipeline determines source budget, allowed families, target-farm bonus, rarity, base, material, affix groups, roll values, law, duplicate protection, and presentation. Each stage uses its own seeded random stream so cosmetic effects cannot change reward outcomes.

Baseline generation completes before separate Spotlight, contract, covenant, or world-state bonus lanes run. Those systems may add a labeled reward but cannot mutate, replace, or reduce the source reward. `CONTENT_PIPELINE.md` defines the schemas, deterministic stage order, quality gates, and statistical validation.

### Target farming

- Every build-defining item lists known source categories after discovery.
- Named enemies and bosses have narrow pools.
- Zone vendors sell rotating direct-purchase items for earned currency.
- The Hearthfold Archive can attune to one desired family, tag, or discovered unique.
- Boss seals accumulate toward a direct choice from that boss's pool.
- Duplicate uniques become **Echo Shards**, used to improve rolls or purchase a missing item.

## 7. Reward caches

Random reward boxes are called **Caches**. They are earned through play, open instantly, occupy no inventory space until opened, and never require a paid key.

Every Cache shows:

- eligible item families and rarity range;
- number of selections or rolls;
- whether duplicates are possible;
- active attunement and pity progress;
- guaranteed rewards;
- the activity that awarded it.

High-tier Caches use a reveal sequence that can be shortened, skipped, replayed from the log, and reduced through accessibility settings. The presentation celebrates the reward without slowing repeated farming.

## 8. Near-unlimited inventory

The player has two inventory contexts.

### Field kit

The Field Kit contains equipped gear, quick-use items, and a generous set of newly collected item slots. It exists for fast comparison and tactical preparation, not weight simulation. Materials and currencies bypass it automatically.

When the Field Kit is full, additional equipment is packed into a recoverable overflow manifest. Drops are never silently destroyed. The player may keep exploring, but cannot equip overflow items until reaching a field terminal, settlement, or Hearthfold anchor.

Materials, currencies, ordinary crafting components, and previously appraised low-rarity duplicates can be collected automatically under player rules. **Field Sweep** is a short, interruptible collection pulse for nearby eligible equipment. It has no real-time charge, inventory-weight penalty, or rarity tax. Combat drops remain in a persistent local manifest long enough to prevent visual clutter without forcing immediate cleanup.

### Archive Vault

The Archive Vault is persistent, searchable, virtualized, and designed to hold an extremely large collection. Capacity upgrades improve indexes, loadout slots, display space, and automation rather than selling a tiny number of basic slots.

Required tools include:

- text search over names, descriptions, powers, and player tags;
- filters for rarity, slot, family, damage, source, zone, level, affix, status, and build tag;
- sort by recent, power, rarity, name, roll quality, source, and duplicate count;
- favorites, locks, custom tags, notes, and saved searches;
- side-by-side comparison with exact stat deltas and law interactions;
- duplicate grouping and "best copy" suggestions;
- configurable auto-salvage rules with a preview and undo ledger;
- named loadouts that reserve items from salvage;
- a collection codex that records discovered variants even after salvage;
- exportable manifest for debugging and community build tools.

The Archive records the first appraisal of every discovered base item. This lets low-rarity joke descriptions remain collectible even after the physical copies are reclaimed.

## 9. The Hearthfold

The Hearthfold is a pocket safe room reached through discovered Underworks anchors. It begins as a one-room shelter and travels as a persistent interior rather than physically walking behind the player.

### Core rules

- Entry is allowed from safe anchors and most out-of-combat wilderness locations.
- Some lairs block entry until a checkpoint is reached. This protects encounter structure without creating long lockouts.
- Returning exits through the same anchor or another unlocked compatible anchor for a modest earned travel cost.
- The Hearthfold is fully safe during ordinary use. Special defense events are clearly opted into and cannot destroy rooms or stored items.

### Room progression

1. **Refuge:** healing, save management, wardrobe, and basic Archive access.
2. **Forge:** equipment upgrades, rerolls, sockets, salvage, and recipe work.
3. **Archive:** advanced search, attunement, collection displays, and item reconstruction.
4. **Training Hall:** loadout practice, damage summaries, respecs, and power trials.
5. **Conservatory:** plants, creatures, materials, ecology research, and passive cultivation.
6. **Guest Wing:** companions, specialists, faction visitors, and divine envoys.
7. **War Room:** zone-state map, contracts, threat forecasts, patrol deployment, and high-Temper configuration.
8. **Threshold Engine:** late-game anchor routing, pocket-dungeon access, and refuge customization.

Rooms have mutually interesting upgrades rather than a single mandatory sequence. Layout and cosmetics are freely rearrangeable.

The Refuge contains a **Reclamation Core** from its first major upgrade. It voluntarily digests selected items into room growth, Salvage, collection progress, and occasional zone-specific materials. The transaction previews everything, protects favorites, locks, loadouts, best copies, and curated rarities, and displays a newly discovered low-rarity item's appraisal before first digestion. Curated uniques remain reconstructable after discovery at a baseline roll.

Every normal room tier is achievable without reclaiming Exotic, Legendary, or Mythic items. Hearthfold progress has no daily contribution cap, offline decay, real-world build time, room damage, or de-leveling. `HEARTHFOLD_UPGRADES.md` defines Core ranks, all eight room trees, cross-room synergies, transaction safety, and slice scope.

## 10. Safety spectrum

The world uses explicit safety states.

- **Sanctuary:** Hearthfold interiors and protected civic cores. Combat and theft are disabled.
- **Safe:** towns and stable camps. Hostile world events do not enter, but local scripted disputes may occur.
- **Semi-safe:** bars, caravan yards, black markets, shrines, and frontier inns. Weapons are usually lowered, healing is available, and direct random attacks are suppressed, but brawls, faction confrontations, scams, duels, and targeted raids can occur after clear warning.
- **Contested:** ordinary open-zone play with patrols and dynamic events.
- **Hostile:** lairs, deep wilderness, enemy strongholds, and boss territories.
- **Cataclysmic:** optional high-Temper spaces with stacked hazards and altered recovery rules, entered only by explicit choice.

HUD iconography and map shading always communicate the current state and its exact rules.

## 11. Living zones

Each zone maintains a compact persistent state rather than simulating every creature continuously.

### State dimensions

- dominant faction and contested subregions;
- settlement prosperity and services;
- threat and elite activity;
- population pressure for enemy families;
- resource abundance and recovery;
- weather, corruption, or magical climate;
- open and blocked routes;
- active public events and recent event cooldowns;
- lair status and boss succession;
- local broadcast attention and divine attention;
- discovered anchors, secrets, and ecology entries.

### Simulation cadence

Immediate combat runs in real time. Nearby encounters update frequently. Distant zone state advances in coarse deterministic steps during travel, explicit rest, authored cross-zone events, and zone load when prior game-time advancement requires it. Closing the game freezes authoritative social and world state. Returning may rebuild indexes and summarize the last committed state, but cannot kill NPCs, expire situations, decay relationships, or worsen a zone.

The layered tick pipeline, named-entity records, activity schedules, relationship and rumor graph, persistence boundary, and scale gate are defined in `WORLD_SIMULATION_AND_MEMORY.md`.

### Change without lockout

The zone may alter roads, patrols, prices, events, visuals, and enemy composition, but critical bosses, rewards, and lore remain replayable through Echo Hunts, rebuilt lairs, memory gates, or successor encounters.

## 12. Living lairs

Important lairs have authored Temperaments and a small persistent mood such as Curious, Bored, Offended, Competitive, Concerned, or Spiteful. Player noise, route, damage variety, negotiation, enemy survival, container behavior, and prior visits can change the mood.

Mood changes room order, hazards, enemy emphasis, dialogue, visual details, and reward category. It does not reduce the total reward budget for a preferred play style. A stealthy, aggressive, cautious, thorough, or strange player should each receive a viable version of the lair.

The vertical-slice lair is **The Courtesy Drain**, an Underworks service tunnel that interprets combat as maintenance requests and issues increasingly hostile ticket updates. It demonstrates that a dungeon personality can exist in signage, architecture, encounter rules, and rewards rather than relying on constant voiceover.

Repeated meaningful clears raise a lair's **Memory Rank**. Higher ranks may reveal a side room, new signature hazard, successor resident, alternate bargain, changed visual motif, or new reward category. Rank advances through completed play and state events, never simply because real-world days passed. It cannot remove earlier unique rewards.

## 13. Settlements and social spaces

Cities and towns are functional progression centers, not long corridors of static dialogue.

Services include vendors, crafting specialties, contracts, faction decisions, rumors, training, transport, bounty boards, companions, cosmetics, minigames, and local zone-state dashboards.

Bars are semi-safe compression points. They provide:

- current rumors grounded in actual zone state;
- hireable specialists and temporary companions;
- food, drink, and build-altering preparation bonuses;
- optional brawls, wagers, performances, and faction encounters;
- a place for the Herald to comment more conversationally without interrupting combat;
- fast travel after discovery, but never exclusive progression purchased with real money.

Named NPCs use authored activity states driven by in-game phase, zone facts, and accepted situations. They do not follow real-world schedules. Essential services retain a proxy, ledger, successor, or alternate source while a character is elsewhere.

The Memory Graph is a local typed query model over relationships, facts, rumors, situations, places, and significant events. It is not a remote social database. Rumors distinguish confirmed facts, credible reports, uncertain claims, and contradictions. Target sources, safety rules, transaction costs, and accessibility information are never rumor-dependent.

`NPC_DOSSIERS.md` defines the character authoring standard and original Gutterbloom cast. `WORLD_SIMULATION_AND_MEMORY.md` defines schedules, knowledge, rumor propagation, relationship edges, and privacy rules.

## 14. Picket, provisional safety inspector

Picket is a knee-high brass surveyor construct found in the Gutterbloom. It marks hazards and weak points, retrieves one nearby drop on a cooldown, equips two side-grade Inspection Protocols, records important in-game events, and disputes the Herald's least helpful announcements.

Picket is the companion and emotional counterweight to the Herald, not a replacement narrator. Trust unlocks field utility, Hearthfold scenes, ecology insight, and character development. Picket can be temporarily disabled in combat but never permanently lost. Its chatter, subtitles, and hazard warnings have separate controls.

## 15. Factions, gods, and demigods

### Mortal factions

Each zone has two to four local groups with compatible and conflicting goals. Reputation unlocks services, variants, contracts, and world-state leverage. It does not require permanently erasing every rival. The player can maintain complicated relationships, and the UI previews important consequences.

### Gods

Gods are powerful intelligences or entities bound into the Expanse. Each embodies a gameplay philosophy and offers a covenant track. A player can maintain contact with all discovered gods but equips only one major covenant and one minor favor at a time.

Gods provide powers with loadout tradeoffs, divine hunts, shrines, envoys, commentary conflicts, and rare relics. **Recognition** is persistent progress and never decays. **Tension** represents interesting disagreement and opens optional challenges, rival offers, mediation, or contradiction routes without reducing baseline rewards, statistics, access, or another relationship.

Covenant changes are allowed at the Hearthfold without permanent locks, lost Recognition, monetary fees, real-world cooldowns, or favor debt. Offerings are voluntary and protected, and every relationship threshold is reachable without sacrificing Exotic, Legendary, or Mythic items. `DIVINE_COVENANTS.md` defines the full relationship contract and first six gods.

### Demigods

Demigods are killable, negotiable, recruitable, or replaceable local powers. Their state influences a zone. Defeating one may create a successor rather than leaving content empty forever.

## 16. The Herald Engine

The Herald Engine is a rule-driven commentary and announcement system. It is not an always-online language model dependency.

### Event inputs

- combat milestones and unusual tactics;
- build composition and law interactions;
- repeated deaths, flawless victories, low-health escapes, and environmental kills;
- legendary drops, duplicates, terrible rolls, and completed sets;
- faction betrayals, rescues, thefts, donations, and bar incidents;
- zone-state changes, first discoveries, hidden routes, and ecological oddities;
- player habits such as hoarding, salvaging, barrel breaking, falling, or refusing divine offers.
- Spotlight changes, Accolades, Indignities, promoted enemies, Picket warnings, and Embarrassment Remnants.

### Selection rules

Each commentary line declares event tags, priority, cooldown, maximum repeats, tone, safety requirement, subtitle duration, interrupt class, and disallowed contexts. A scheduler selects a line using weighted deterministic variety, typed Comedy Memories, and a global novelty and interruption budget. Grammar-constrained authored fragments add breadth, while important scenes and callbacks remain fully authored.

The implementation is a local typed event and definition system. It requires no remote language model, Redis service, account, or server-side player-history database. `COMMENTARY_SPOTLIGHT_SPEC.md` is authoritative for schemas, hard filters, scoring, deterministic selection, retention, and performance budgets.

### Channels

- **Critical:** boss transitions, evacuation warnings, major state changes. Cannot be disabled visually, but voice can be muted.
- **Announcement:** legendary drops, zone victories, divine challenges, rare events.
- **Commentary:** reactive humor and observations.
- **Ambient:** settlement broadcasts, bar conversations, and distant world texture.

All spoken content appears in a searchable history. Players can independently control Herald and Picket volume, subtitles, frequency, profanity intensity, teasing intensity, and streamer-safe variants. Quiet, Default, and Unhinged presets change ordinary humor density without changing critical information or rewards.

## 17. Accolades, Indignities, and promoted enemies

Accolades recognize impressive or unusual play. Indignities remember mechanically specific failures. They award cosmetics, Spotlight, Caches, callbacks, bar gossip, recovery objectives, or promoted enemies rather than broad permanent power.

An ordinary enemy that causes a memorable defeat can receive a name, title, compatible mutation, tiny ceremonial promotion marker, bonus reward pool, and limited behavioral memory. Defeating it resolves the local incident. It may later return only as an Echo Hunt, story, or successor influence, so the joke creates content without stalking the player forever.

Full voice, example achievements, callback-memory rules, and production budgets are defined in `HUMOR_AND_VOICE.md`.

## 18. Quests, contracts, and events

The game avoids a single mandatory campaign funnel.

- **Zone threads:** authored local problem chains that introduce places and systems.
- **Contracts:** repeatable, targetable objectives with known reward categories.
- **World events:** state-driven encounters that can appear in multiple valid locations.
- **Echo Hunts:** replayable versions of named encounters with optional modifiers.
- **Ecology work:** study, capture, redirect, cultivate, or cull populations.
- **Faction operations:** change control or services without permanently deleting opposing content.
- **Divine trials:** build constraints and rule twists with clear opt-in rewards.
- **Rumors:** lightweight discoveries, caches, unusual vendors, or environmental stories.

Objectives do not expire while active. Rotations add opportunity but do not remove unique rewards; rotation rewards enter other targetable sources afterward.

The Situation Engine selects zero or more authored opportunities from dirty world facts. It hard-filters prerequisites, duplication, participant conflicts, active budgets, content preservation, and presentation load before deterministic scoring. Unaccepted situations may reveal clues, move, become dormant, or transform, but cannot kill a named NPC offscreen or remove essential content. Accepted objectives stop autonomous expiration. `SITUATION_ENGINE.md` defines categories, lifecycle, scoring, discovery, outcomes, recovery, and tests.

## 19. Economy and crafting

The economy uses a small number of clear currencies:

- **Marks:** normal buying, selling, settlement services, and optional anchor travel.
- **Salvage:** common crafting and base upgrades.
- **Essences:** elemental and power modification.
- **Echo Shards:** duplicate unique and legendary conversion.
- **Boss Seals:** source-specific direct-purchase protection.
- **Recognition:** separate persistent relationship ledger for each god, never sold, spent, or reduced by inactivity.

Crafting improves and redirects loot rather than replacing exploration. It can upgrade quality, tune affixes, move a good law to a compatible chassis at high cost, add sockets, create consumables, and reconstruct discovered low-tier items. It cannot print undiscovered Mythic Relics.

Major voluntary item sinks are Hearthfold Reclamation, crafting, divine offerings, settlement projects, and a late transparent Probability Engine. All show exact inputs, protected items, possible outputs, odds where relevant, and whether reconstruction remains available. No sink activates because storage filled.

## 20. Death, recovery, and failure

On defeat:

- the player returns to the nearest valid anchor;
- equipped and stored items remain intact;
- every completed **Spotlight** rank reward remains banked;
- consumables actually used remain spent;
- the encounter resets to a fair checkpoint;
- an optional **Embarrassment Remnant** stores active progress above the last banked threshold at a safe reachable place near the defeat, but never contains owned gear;
- a qualifying ordinary killer may be promoted into a named local champion with extra loot;
- the Herald may comment subject to the player's teasing settings and repeat cooldown.

Recovering the remnant restores all active progress. Ignoring it converts half of that progress into consolation Cache progress at an anchor. Only a separately accepted and previewed `Do It Live` complication can wager one banked rank reward. Failure can alter a local event outcome or faction state, but important rewards remain attainable through another route.

## 21. Long-tail play

Endgame is a set of overlapping pursuits rather than one infinitely scaling corridor:

- complete zone ledgers;
- perfect or transform favorite legendary items;
- master Disciplines and Advanced Paths;
- climb optional World Temper and mutator combinations;
- hunt divine relics and demigod successors;
- rebuild zone states for specific encounters;
- expand and decorate the Hearthfold;
- complete collections and unusual build challenges;
- play optional archived seeded challenge boards without affecting ordinary saves or losing access when a new seed is featured;
- play future zones without invalidating old equipment through runaway item-level inflation.
